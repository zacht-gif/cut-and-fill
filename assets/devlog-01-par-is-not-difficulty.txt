Suggested devlog title:  Par is not difficulty

========================================================================


Every level in Cut & Fill ships with a par that a solver has proved is the
true optimum. Getting there was straightforward. Using par to order the
campaign was the mistake.

Par tells you how long a level is. I had assumed that was a decent stand-in
for how hard it is, and ordered the first chapter that way. It played badly
and I could not say why.

So I measured two things par cannot see.

[GIF]

Trap density is the share of reachable positions from which the site can no
longer be finished. It is the "I ruined this twenty moves ago and didn't
notice" axis. Dirt drops into a hole permanently, so a wasted load can strand
you with no way back and no signal that anything went wrong.

Lethality is the share of available moves that end the run outright — sinking,
being run over, or leaving dirt in a live lane for a truck to pile into. That
one is invisible to trap density, because a fatal move never becomes a
reachable position to count.

Both are computed exactly rather than sampled: enumerate every reachable
position, mark the ones with a winning move, then sweep that backwards through
the move graph until it settles. Whatever is left cannot reach a win.

The numbers said par was not merely a weak proxy for difficulty. It was
inverted.

The three longest levels in chapter one were the three safest, at 16 to 35
percent trap. A five-move tutorial sat at 64 percent. That is not noise, it is
the shape of the game: long levels are open rooms with space to recover, while
short ones are tight single-load puzzles where most mistakes are quietly
fatal. Ordering by par had been actively making the curve worse.

The campaign is now ordered by trap times par — roughly, the effort you expect
to lose to a ruined run. Neither number works alone. A four-move level can be
trap-dense and still painless, because restarting costs you nothing.

Measuring also caught two tutorials that were punishing players for learning
the mechanic the tutorial was there to teach. Each handed out exactly one load
of dirt, so the first misstep was unrecoverable:

Corner Work went from 64 to 45 percent trap.
Soft Ground went from 72 to 38 percent, and par 7 to 6.

The first spare load I added to Soft Ground was useless, and it looked fine. It
sat on the bottom row, where nothing can be pushed up into the goal's row — a
spare that is not spare. The measurement is what caught that too.

None of this changes a single rule. The levels you play are the same levels.
They just arrive in an order that was measured instead of guessed, and the two
that taught by punishing you no longer do.
